# TinyTots User Frontend — Homepage

## Purpose

The homepage is the primary brand and discovery experience.

It must combine premium fashion editorial direction with a clear path into commerce.

## Primary Objectives

1. Establish TinyTots visual identity immediately.
2. Present the current campaign or strongest brand story.
3. Introduce products without becoming a generic catalog.
4. Build trust.
5. Drive discovery into collections and products.

## Page Hierarchy

Recommended conceptual sequence:

```text
GLOBAL HEADER
↓
HERO / CAMPAIGN
↓
NEW / FEATURED COLLECTION
↓
EDITORIAL STORY
↓
PRODUCT DISCOVERY
↓
BRAND / QUALITY STORY
↓
TRUST / SERVICE
↓
NEWSLETTER / COMMUNITY
↓
FOOTER
```

Exact sections must be driven by current campaigns and available content.

## Hero

The hero is the highest-impact visual area.

Requirements:

- strong art direction
- clear headline
- concise supporting copy where needed
- one primary CTA
- optional secondary CTA
- readable navigation
- responsive composition

Possible enhancement:

- controlled 3D
- image motion
- cinematic reveal

3D must follow Phase 05.

## Product Introduction

Use curated product groups rather than displaying a random catalog.

Possible labels:

- New Arrivals
- Featured
- Best Sellers
- Collection

Only use labels supported by actual data/business logic.

## Editorial Sections

Editorial content should create brand desire and explain the world of TinyTots.

Avoid excessive text density.

## Motion

Potential:

- hero reveal
- image reveal
- subtle parallax
- product card hover
- scroll choreography

Do not animate every section.

## Mobile

The mobile homepage is an intentional composition.

Do not simply stack desktop sections.

## Performance

Above-the-fold content receives priority.

Heavy 3D and below-fold assets must not block initial usability.

## Definition of Done

A first-time visitor understands:

- what TinyTots is
- what is being presented
- where to shop
- why the brand feels premium

within the initial viewport and first scroll sequence.
