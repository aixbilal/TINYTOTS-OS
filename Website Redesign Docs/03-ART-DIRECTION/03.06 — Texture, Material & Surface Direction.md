# TinyTots Texture, Material & Surface Direction

## 1. Philosophy

Texture should make the digital experience feel tactile without becoming skeuomorphic.

## 2. Preferred Material References

Visual inspiration may come from:

- cotton
- linen
- paper
- brushed stone
- matte ceramic
- natural wood
- soft wool

## 3. Digital Translation

Use material cues primarily through photography and imagery.

UI texture should be extremely subtle.

## 4. Paper

Potential use:

- editorial backgrounds
- campaign storytelling
- printed-look content

Do not make the entire site resemble paper.

## 5. Fabric

Fabric texture may appear in:

- close-up photography
- campaign visuals
- product details

Do not overlay fabric textures across normal UI.

## 6. Grain

Very subtle grain may be explored for selected cinematic sections.

It must:

- remain barely perceptible
- not reduce text clarity
- not create excessive rendering cost

## 7. Blur

Blur is an atmospheric tool, not a default surface treatment.

## 8. Shadows

Natural-looking shadows are preferred to dramatic floating UI shadows.

## 9. Material Principle

If users notice the texture before the product/content, it is too strong.

## 10. Acceptance Criteria

Material effects must remain subordinate to typography, photography, and product information.
