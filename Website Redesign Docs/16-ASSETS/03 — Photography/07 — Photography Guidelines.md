# TinyTots — Photography Guidelines

**Document:** Photography Guidelines  
**Location:** `16-ASSETS/03 — Photography/07 — Photography Guidelines.md`  
**Status:** Approved Photography Standard  
**Applies To:** Website, campaigns, product presentation, editorial content, social content, signage and future TinyTots visual production.

---

# 1. Purpose

This document defines the visual and technical rules governing TinyTots photography.

The objective is not to force every photograph to look identical.

The objective is to ensure that every photograph feels as though it belongs to the same TinyTots world.

The approved photography library is divided into:

1. Brand Photography
2. Lifestyle Photography
3. Product Photography
4. Editorial Photography
5. Campaign Photography
6. Detail Photography

These categories perform different functions but share one visual identity.

---

# 2. Core Photography Philosophy

TinyTots photography should feel:

- Warm
- Natural
- Premium
- Calm
- Childhood-centered
- Tactile
- Editorial
- Timeless
- Human
- Refined without feeling sterile

Photography should avoid the appearance of:

- Generic stock photography
- Artificial children's advertising
- Oversaturated e-commerce imagery
- Cheap studio photography
- Excessively staged poses
- Cluttered environments
- Heavy digital effects
- Artificially perfect environments
- Excessive branding

The visual world should communicate:

> Beautiful clothes made for real childhood moments.

---

# 3. Master Visual Direction

The approved photographic language established by the TinyTots reference library combines:

- warm natural light
- cream and ivory foundations
- natural wood
- linen and cotton
- muted earth tones
- restrained botanical elements
- subtle floral patterns
- wicker and woven materials
- Mediterranean and Scandinavian influences
- soft architectural environments
- natural childhood interaction

The brand should feel premium without appearing formal or inaccessible.

---

# 4. Color Direction

## Primary photographic colors

Use predominantly:

- Ivory
- Warm white
- Cream
- Oatmeal
- Beige
- Sand
- Camel
- Natural wood
- Kraft
- Muted olive
- Sage

## Secondary colors

Controlled use is encouraged for:

- Dusty blue
- Muted yellow
- Blush
- Soft peach
- Burgundy
- Denim
- Muted green
- Restrained floral colors

TinyTots must not become an entirely beige brand.

Neutral environments should allow the clothing and childhood elements to introduce controlled color.

---

# 5. Lighting

Natural-looking light is the default.

Preferred lighting:

- Window light
- Soft morning light
- Late-afternoon light
- Golden-hour light
- Diffused daylight
- Directional sunlight with controlled shadows

Light should produce visible depth.

Avoid completely flat illumination.

---

## 5.1 Shadows

Shadows are part of the TinyTots photographic language.

Acceptable:

- Window shadows
- Foliage shadows
- Architectural shadows
- Soft garment folds
- Natural object shadows

Avoid:

- Harsh flash shadows
- Artificial HDR
- Extremely crushed blacks
- Excessive contrast

---

# 6. White Balance

Photography should remain slightly warm.

Skin must remain natural.

Cream clothing must not become:

- bright yellow
- orange
- grey
- pure digital white

Warmth should come primarily from lighting, environment and materials rather than aggressive post-processing.

---

# 7. Exposure & Contrast

Images should generally be:

- bright
- soft
- dimensional
- naturally contrasted

Highlights should retain texture.

Dark areas should preserve detail.

Avoid:

- clipped whites
- crushed blacks
- heavy HDR
- excessive clarity
- artificial sharpening

---

# 8. Composition

TinyTots photography should use intentional composition.

Preferred techniques:

- generous negative space
- asymmetrical compositions
- layered depth
- architectural framing
- foreground foliage
- natural leading lines
- centered product compositions when appropriate
- rule-of-thirds portrait placement
- environmental context

Not every photograph should center its subject.

---

# 9. Negative Space

Negative space is particularly important for:

- Hero photography
- Campaign photography
- Editorial photography
- Signage
- Marketing banners

When photography may receive typography or UI overlays, deliberately preserve clean areas of the composition.

Do not permanently bake interface elements into reusable photography.

---

# 10. Camera Perspective

Use varied perspectives across a collection.

Recommended:

- Eye level
- Child eye level
- Slight low angle
- Environmental wide shot
- Medium portrait
- Close portrait
- Overhead product shot
- Detail macro

Avoid producing an entire library from one camera position.

---

# 11. Children & Posing

Children should appear:

- comfortable
- curious
- playful
- calm
- connected
- naturally expressive

Preferred actions include:

- Walking
- Running
- Reading
- Drawing
- Playing
- Exploring
- Talking
- Holding hands
- Looking at another child
- Quiet observation

Avoid excessive:

- fashion-model posing
- forced smiles
- unnatural hand positions
- adult-like styling
- glamour posing

Childhood should remain the emotional center.

---

# 12. Model Variety

The visual library should not repeatedly depict what appears to be the same child unless the images intentionally belong to one campaign shoot.

Across the broader brand library, use variation in:

- age
- appearance
- hairstyle
- wardrobe
- group composition
- environments

The purpose is visual authenticity and breadth.

---

# 13. Wardrobe Styling

Wardrobe should feel:

- comfortable
- premium
- age appropriate
- tactile
- easy to move in

Preferred materials:

- Cotton
- Linen
- Knitwear
- Ribbed fabrics
- Soft denim
- Embroidery
- Eyelet
- Natural-texture blends

Avoid excessive accessories.

The clothing must remain the hero.

---

# 14. Brand Photography

Brand Photography establishes the TinyTots physical world.

Subjects may include:

- Packaging
- Garments
- Clothing rails
- Brand materials
- Workspace
- Fabric
- Tags
- Boxes
- Tissue paper
- Store environment

Branding should appear naturally.

GOOD:

Logo printed on a physical box.

GOOD:

TinyTots garment label.

GOOD:

Branded shopping bag.

AVOID:

Artificial advertising headline floating over a reusable photograph.

---

# 15. Lifestyle Photography

Lifestyle Photography captures childhood.

The photograph should communicate a moment rather than merely display clothing.

Approved themes include:

- Indoor play
- Outdoor play
- Reading
- Drawing
- Sibling interaction
- Exploration
- Movement
- Quiet moments

Lifestyle images should feel candid even when art-directed.

---

# 16. Product Photography

Product Photography exists to clearly communicate the garment.

Primary objectives:

1. Accurate silhouette
2. Accurate color
3. Visible material
4. Visible construction
5. Clear product identity

Useful presentations include:

- Front
- Back
- Flat lay
- Hanging
- Folded
- Coordinated outfit
- Styled product composition

Product photography must not depend on campaign typography.

---

# 17. Editorial Photography

Editorial Photography permits stronger artistic direction.

It may use:

- Architecture
- Dramatic negative space
- Unusual cropping
- Groups
- Environmental portraiture
- Nature
- Stronger composition
- Cinematic framing

Editorial photography should remain recognizably TinyTots while allowing greater visual experimentation.

---

# 18. Campaign Photography

Campaign Photography represents a specific collection, season or story.

Campaign photography may be more explicitly branded.

Typical campaign sequence:

1. Hero
2. Pair
3. Group
4. Movement
5. Campaign story
6. Closing image

Campaign photography should have greater visual impact than general lifestyle photography.

---

# 19. Detail Photography

Detail Photography communicates craftsmanship.

Capture:

- Fabric weave
- Print
- Stitching
- Buttons
- Embroidery
- Labels
- Collars
- Ruffles
- Cuffs
- Seams
- Hems
- Closures
- Construction

Use genuine macro or close-up framing.

Do not simply crop a normal product photograph and call it detail photography.

---

# 20. Backgrounds

Preferred environments:

- Warm plaster
- Natural stone
- Linen
- Neutral studio surfaces
- Natural wood
- Gardens
- Meadows
- Mediterranean architecture
- Calm domestic interiors
- Boutique environments

Avoid:

- Random stock-photo environments
- Neon environments
- Artificial gradients
- Busy commercial malls
- Highly saturated children's sets
- Distracting clutter

---

# 21. Props

Props must support the scene.

Approved examples:

- Books
- Wooden toys
- Wicker baskets
- Flowers
- Plants
- Ceramics
- Linen
- Packaging
- Wooden furniture
- Garment racks

Props must never overpower the clothing or child.

---

# 22. Botanical Elements

Botanical elements are part of the established visual language.

Preferred:

- Olive-like foliage
- Eucalyptus
- Dried flowers
- Meadow flowers
- Restrained greenery

Do not place plants into every composition.

Botanical elements should remain optional rather than becoming a mandatory visual cliché.

---

# 23. Typography Inside Photography

Reusable photography should generally contain no artificial typography.

Physical typography is acceptable when naturally present on:

- Packaging
- Labels
- Tags
- Signage
- Printed collateral

Campaign artwork may include typography when the asset is intentionally designed as a finished campaign composition.

Source photography should remain clean whenever possible.

---

# 24. Cropping

Every important shoot should consider multiple placements.

## Landscape

Recommended for:

- Homepage hero
- Campaign banners
- Editorial sections
- Signage

## Portrait

Recommended for:

- Editorial cards
- Mobile
- Social media
- Product storytelling

## Square / Near-square

Recommended for:

- Product grids
- Cards
- Social content
- Supporting modules

Never place critical visual information directly against the edge of the frame.

---

# 25. Desktop and Mobile

Do not assume a desktop crop will automatically work on mobile.

Hero and campaign photography should preserve:

- safe subject areas
- negative space
- flexible cropping zones

Where necessary, create dedicated mobile variants.

---

# 26. Resolution

Master photography should be retained at the highest practical resolution.

Web exports should be generated separately.

Never overwrite master photography with compressed web versions.

---

# 27. File Formats

Recommended master formats:

- TIFF where appropriate
- High-quality PNG where appropriate
- Maximum-quality JPEG for photographic masters

Recommended web formats:

- AVIF
- WebP

JPEG may be retained as a compatibility fallback where necessary.

---

# 28. Image Optimization

Website photography must be optimized before production delivery.

Optimization should consider:

- rendered dimensions
- device pixel ratio
- responsive images
- lazy loading
- preload priority
- compression
- image format
- CDN delivery

Do not deliver a multi-megabyte master photograph directly to a normal product card.

---

# 29. Retouching

Retouching should remain restrained.

Allowed:

- Exposure correction
- White-balance correction
- Minor color correction
- Dust removal
- Minor background cleanup
- Crop adjustment
- Lens correction

Avoid:

- Plastic skin
- Extreme smoothing
- Artificial body modification
- Unrealistic garment reshaping
- Excessive saturation
- Artificial HDR

---

# 30. AI-Generated Photography

AI-generated photography may be used for:

- Art direction
- Design references
- Concept campaigns
- Placeholder imagery
- Mood exploration
- Non-factual brand storytelling where appropriate

AI imagery must not falsely represent:

- An actual product
- Exact fabric construction
- Exact product color
- A real store
- A real customer
- A real business event

when those details have not been verified.

For actual e-commerce product listings, real product photography remains the source of truth.

---

# 31. Product Accuracy

Product imagery used for purchasing decisions must accurately represent the item being sold.

AI-generated imagery must not invent:

- Buttons
- Embroidery
- Pockets
- Labels
- Fabric
- Prints
- Colors
- Fit
- Construction details

and present those inventions as factual product information.

---

# 32. Accessibility

Photography must support accessible interfaces.

Important considerations:

- Maintain sufficient contrast for overlaid text.
- Avoid placing important text over visually noisy areas.
- Supply meaningful alt text in implementation.
- Decorative imagery should use appropriate empty alt treatment.
- Do not communicate essential information exclusively through photography.

---

# 33. Photography and UI

Photography supports the interface.

It does not replace interface hierarchy.

UI elements such as:

- Price
- Product name
- Buttons
- Badges
- Navigation
- Form controls

should normally remain actual interface components rather than being permanently embedded in photographs.

---

# 34. Consistency Without Repetition

Consistency means maintaining:

- Light
- Mood
- quality
- material language
- art direction

It does NOT mean repeatedly generating:

- the same beige room
- the same child
- the same plant
- the same garment
- the same pose
- the same camera angle

TinyTots should have a recognizable world, not one endlessly repeated scene.

# 36. Final Photography Checklist

Before approving a photograph, confirm:

- [ ] Does it feel like TinyTots?
- [ ] Is the lighting natural and controlled?
- [ ] Is the color treatment appropriate?
- [ ] Is the clothing clearly visible where required?
- [ ] Is the composition intentional?
- [ ] Is the environment uncluttered?
- [ ] Are props restrained?
- [ ] Does it perform a clear asset role?
- [ ] Does it add something different to the library?
- [ ] Is branding natural rather than forced?
- [ ] Can the image crop effectively?
- [ ] Is there adequate resolution?
- [ ] Is a mobile crop possible where required?
- [ ] Is product information factually accurate?
- [ ] Has unnecessary AI-generated text been avoided?
- [ ] Is the master preserved separately from web exports?

---

# 37. Governing Principle

TinyTots photography should never chase visual complexity for its own sake.

The strongest image is the one that communicates:

**childhood + clothing + warmth + quality**

with the least unnecessary visual noise.
