# TinyTots — Video Guidelines

**Document:** Video Guidelines  
**Location:** `16-ASSETS/04 — Video/07 — Video Guidelines.md`  
**Status:** Approved Video Standard  
**Applies To:** TinyTots website, mobile website, campaigns, editorial storytelling, social adaptations and future digital experiences.

---

# 1. Purpose

This document defines the visual, technical and operational standards for the TinyTots video system.

It governs:

- Hero videos
- Campaign videos
- Product videos
- Editorial videos
- Background videos
- Mobile video variants
- Video optimization
- Website implementation
- Storage and repository handling
- Future video production

The objective is to maintain a consistent TinyTots motion language while preventing unnecessary video production, misleading product representation and excessive website performance cost.

---

# 2. Current TinyTots Position

TinyTots currently operates as a children's clothing **retailer/shop**.

TinyTots is not currently treated as a complete private-label clothing manufacturer.

Therefore, video content may communicate:

- Children's fashion
- Retail experience
- Childhood
- Family warmth
- Shopping
- Packaging
- Seasonal styling
- Editorial storytelling

Video content must not falsely imply that every garment shown is designed or manufactured by TinyTots.

# 6. Video Visual DNA

All TinyTots lifestyle/editorial video should generally belong to the same visual world.

Core characteristics include:

- Warm ivory
- Cream
- Natural wood
- Deep forest green
- Restrained terracotta
- Soft daylight
- Calm environments
- Contemporary children's fashion
- Natural childhood behavior
- Premium editorial composition
- Controlled camera movement
- Generous negative space

Not every frame needs every brand color.

The palette should feel naturally integrated rather than artificially imposed.

---

# 7. Motion Character

TinyTots motion should generally feel:

- Calm
- Controlled
- Premium
- Human
- Warm
- Observational
- Natural
- Refined

Preferred camera behavior includes:

- Slow dolly movement
- Gentle tracking
- Restrained parallax
- Static compositions with natural subject movement
- Controlled close-ups
- Natural depth of field

Avoid:

- Aggressive zooming
- Excessive handheld shake
- Rapid cuts
- Flash transitions
- Social-template effects
- Constant camera movement
- Artificial speed ramps
- Excessive slow motion

---

# 8. Children in Video

Children should behave like children.

Preferred behavior includes:

- Walking
- Reading
- Playing
- Sharing
- Exploring
- Quiet laughter
- Looking at another child
- Natural interaction with objects
- Small spontaneous gestures

Avoid:

- Adult runway behavior
- Provocative or age-inappropriate styling
- Highly choreographed model poses
- Forced synchronized movement
- Constant direct eye contact with camera
- Artificial expressions
- Unrealistic physical behavior

Children must always be portrayed appropriately and respectfully.

---

# 9. Clothing Representation

TinyTots currently operates as a retailer.

Therefore, garments shown in generated videos should generally appear as realistic multi-brand retail merchandise.

Do not automatically generate:

- TinyTots garment labels
- TinyTots embroidery
- TinyTots shoe logos
- TinyTots monograms on clothing
- Fictional TinyTots designer collections

TinyTots branding may appropriately appear on:

- Shopping bags
- Packaging
- Gift cards
- Retail signage
- Website environments
- Retail collateral

---

# 10. Product Accuracy

AI-generated lifestyle videos must not be presented as exact representations of products available for purchase unless the depicted product has been verified against actual inventory.

This distinction is critical.

Lifestyle video:

Represents mood, retail positioning and storytelling.

Product video:

Represents a specific sellable product.

These are not interchangeable.

---

# 11. Product Videos

`03 — Product Videos/` is intentionally reserved at the current stage.

Future product videos should preferably be created using:

- Real TinyTots inventory
- Real product photography
- Real product footage
- Verified colors
- Verified details
- Verified garment construction

Product video must accurately represent the item customers will receive.

Do not use generative video to invent details of a real sellable product.

---

# 12. Hero Videos

Hero video exists to support the homepage message.

It should not overpower:

- Headline
- Supporting copy
- CTA
- Navigation
- Product discovery

The hero video is environmental storytelling, not the entire interface.

HV01 is the current reference for this category.

---

# 13. Hero Negative Space

Hero compositions should intentionally reserve visual space for live website content.

Do not permanently render:

- Headline
- CTA
- Price
- Promotional badge
- Navigation
- Tagline

inside the master video.

The website should render these as actual interface elements.

Benefits include:

- Responsive layouts
- Accessibility
- Localization
- Easier editing
- Campaign flexibility
- Better typography
- Better SEO semantics

---

# 14. Campaign Videos

Campaign videos may have more visual energy than hero or editorial videos.

However, they should remain within the TinyTots visual system.

Campaign video may emphasize:

- Seasonal styling
- Outfit combinations
- New arrivals
- Shopping moments
- Fashion details
- Movement

Campaign video should not automatically imply that TinyTots manufactures the clothing shown.

---

# 15. Evergreen vs Campaign-Specific Assets

Maintain a distinction between:

## Evergreen

Reusable across multiple periods.

Examples:

- HV01
- EV01
- BV01

## Campaign Specific

Connected to:

- Eid
- Summer
- Winter
- Back to School
- Sale
- New collection
- Special event

Do not unnecessarily add dates, discount percentages or seasonal text directly into master video files.

Live interface/marketing typography should handle such messaging where practical.

---

# 16. Editorial Videos

Editorial video exists primarily for storytelling.

It may communicate:

- Childhood
- Family
- Discovery
- Play
- Everyday moments
- Trust
- Warmth

The clothing should remain aesthetically strong, but editorial video does not need to behave like a product advertisement.

EV01 is the current reference.

---

# 17. Background Videos

Background video must remain subordinate to website content.

BV01 establishes this category.

Background motion should:

- Be subtle
- Loop smoothly
- Maintain negative space
- Avoid rapid visual change
- Avoid embedded typography
- Avoid distracting contrast changes
- Remain understandable without sound

Abstract background video should never become more visually dominant than the content placed over it.

---

# 18. Seamless Looping

Background loops require particular attention.

A seamless loop should avoid obvious changes between the end and beginning.

Check:

- Object position
- Rotation
- Scale
- Camera position
- Lighting
- Shadow direction
- Background
- Motion velocity

If an otherwise approved generated asset has a small looping problem, fixing the loop in video post-production is preferable to repeatedly regenerating the entire visual concept.

Primary desktop landscape target:

**16:9**

Preferred production target:

**1920×1080 or higher-quality equivalent before optimization**

Current generated desktop assets are:

**1280×720**

They remain approved visual masters/concepts.

Higher-resolution production versions should be obtained or prepared where practical before final public deployment.

---

# 20. Mobile Format

Primary mobile target:

**9:16**

Preferred production target:

**1080×1920**

Current generated mobile assets are:

**720×1280**

They remain approved visual masters.

Do not regenerate approved videos solely to increase pixel dimensions unless actual website testing demonstrates insufficient visual quality or a higher-quality generation/export path is available.

---

# 21. Mobile Composition

Mobile video must be designed for vertical viewing.

Prioritize:

- Central subject placement
- Faces
- Hands
- Product/fabric details
- Child interaction
- Vertical environmental depth
- Safe areas for interface content

Avoid putting essential information at extreme edges.

Do not assume a landscape center crop will always produce a good mobile experience.

---

# 22. Desktop and Mobile Relationship

Desktop and mobile variants belong to the same concept family.

Example:

HV01
→ MV01

CV01
→ MV02

EV01
→ MV03

BV01
→ MV04

They do not need to be frame-for-frame identical.

They should preserve:

- Purpose
- Emotional direction
- Visual DNA
- Retail positioning
- Brand palette
- General storytelling concept

Composition may change significantly to suit the different aspect ratio.

---

# 23. Frame Rate

The current approved video system uses:

**24 fps**

This suits the restrained cinematic direction.

Future assets do not need a higher frame rate unless a specific motion requirement justifies it.

Consistency is generally preferable.

---

# 24. Duration

Current videos are approximately:

**10 seconds**

This is appropriate for short web motion.

Typical future ranges:

Hero:
approximately 8–15 seconds

Background loop:
approximately 6–15 seconds

Campaign:
approximately 8–30 seconds depending on placement

Editorial:
variable according to storytelling requirements

Product:
variable according to product demonstration requirements

These are working ranges rather than absolute limits.

---

# 25. Audio

Current website masters do not require audio.

Autoplay website videos should be designed to work completely without sound.

Do not make essential information dependent on:

- Dialogue
- Voiceover
- Music
- Sound effects

Campaign/social versions may later receive audio separately where appropriate.

---

# 26. Autoplay

When video is used as ambient website motion, implementation should generally support:

- muted playback
- autoplay where browser policy permits
- looping where appropriate
- inline playback
- poster/fallback imagery

Do not rely on autoplay as the only way a user can understand important information.

---

# 27. Reduced Motion

Users who prefer reduced motion should not be forced to consume decorative animation.

Where appropriate, the website should respond to:

`prefers-reduced-motion`

Possible behavior:

- Show poster image
- Pause background animation
- Avoid autoplay
- Replace motion with static composition

Accessibility takes priority over decorative motion.

---

# 28. Poster Images

Important website videos should have poster/fallback imagery.

Possible naming:

HV01-P — Homepage Lifestyle Hero Poster.webp

CV01-P — Seasonal Retail Campaign Poster.webp

EV01-P — Childhood & Story Poster.webp

BV01-P — Brand Motion Poster.webp

Posters may be derived from approved video frames or separately prepared where needed.

Choose frames that remain visually useful without motion.

---

# 29. Loading Strategy

Video must not unnecessarily delay meaningful page content.

Prioritize:

1. Page structure
2. Typography
3. Navigation
4. Critical imagery/content
5. Video enhancement

Video is an enhancement, not a reason for the homepage to feel slow.

---

# 30. Compression

Production video should be optimized before website deployment.

The master generation/export should not automatically be used directly as the final web file.

Maintain:

MASTER
↓
PRODUCTION TRANSCODE
↓
WEB DELIVERY

Compression must balance:

- Visual quality
- File size
- Startup time
- Mobile bandwidth
- Device performance

Exact bitrate should be determined during implementation/testing rather than permanently invented in this document.

---

# 31. Web Formats

Where implementation supports it, prepare appropriate web-delivery formats.

Possible formats include:

- WebM
- MP4

Browser/device compatibility must determine final delivery strategy.

Do not preserve a large editing/master codec merely because it has higher theoretical quality.

---

# 32. Master vs Production Export

Maintain a strict distinction.

## Master

Highest-quality approved source.

## Production Export

Compressed/optimized website version.

Example:

HV01 Master
↓
HV01 Desktop WebM
HV01 Desktop MP4
HV01 Poster WebP

MV01 Master
↓
MV01 Mobile WebM
MV01 Mobile MP4
MV01 Poster WebP

Never treat a heavily compressed web export as the archival master.

---

# 33. GitHub Policy

Large video master files should not be pushed into the normal TinyTots application Git repository.

This includes, where practical:

- MP4 masters
- MOV masters
- Large WebM masters
- Raw footage
- Editing project files
- Large intermediate renders

GitHub remains appropriate for:

- Documentation
- Video manifests
- Implementation code
- Small configuration files
- References
- Asset IDs
- Usage rules

---

# 34. External Master Storage

Large video masters should be stored outside the primary application repository.

The documentation system should still record:

- Asset ID
- Asset name
- Category
- Status
- Aspect ratio
- Resolution
- Duration
- Usage
- Master location
- Production location
- Mobile relationship
- Notes

This allows the documentation to remain the source of truth without making Git history a media archive.

---

# 35. Website Delivery

The website should consume optimized production video rather than raw archival masters.

Conceptually:

MASTER STORAGE
      ↓
TRANSCODE / OPTIMIZE
      ↓
WEB MEDIA STORAGE / CDN
      ↓
TINYTOTS WEBSITE

The exact hosting provider may be selected during implementation.

Do not hard-code an unverified provider into the design specification.

# 38. AI-Generated Video

Generative video may be used for:

- Lifestyle storytelling
- Editorial concepts
- Abstract brand motion
- Campaign concepts
- Non-product-specific atmosphere

Generative video should not be used to falsely represent exact merchandise characteristics.

---

# 39. AI Artifact QA

Every generated video must be inspected before approval.

Check for:

- Malformed hands
- Extra fingers
- Facial distortion
- Changing facial identity
- Duplicated children
- Clothing mutations
- Impossible garment movement
- Floating objects
- Broken shadows
- Unnatural walking
- Object disappearance
- Sudden environment changes
- Random text
- Fake logos
- Watermarks/generator marks
- Loop discontinuities

Do not approve solely from the first frame.

Review the complete video.

---

# 40. Generator Marks and Watermarks

Generator-interface marks, watermarks or unwanted generated symbols are not part of the TinyTots identity.

Final production video must use clean exports permitted by the relevant generation platform and its terms.

Do not deliberately hide or remove required attribution in violation of platform terms.

---

# 41. Text Inside Video

Default rule:

**No permanent website UI text inside master video.**

Avoid baking:

- Headlines
- Buttons
- Navigation
- Prices
- Discount percentages
- Product names
- Seasonal dates

into evergreen master video.

Use live website content whenever practical.

---

# 42. Branding Frequency

TinyTots does not need its logo in every video frame.

Brand recognition can come from:

- Website context
- Palette
- Art direction
- Packaging
- Typography
- Geometric graphics
- Overall consistency

Repeated logo placement can make premium content feel less refined.

---

# 43. TinyTots Logo

If the TinyTots logo appears in video, the existing verified geometric mark remains authoritative.

Do not:

- Invent alternate TinyTots logos
- Distort the mark
- Generate approximate versions as master branding
- Replace the established mark with AI interpretations

If exact logo fidelity is important, add the verified logo during post-production rather than relying on generative rendering.

---

# 44. Seasonal Video Strategy

Do not generate a new campaign video for every minor promotion.

Generate new seasonal video when it creates meaningful value.

Examples may include:

- Eid
- Summer
- Winter
- Back to School
- Major new arrivals campaign

Smaller promotions can reuse existing footage with live website campaign messaging.

---

# 45. Future Real Photography/Video

The current generated video system establishes art direction.

When TinyTots later conducts real professional shoots, these assets can function as references for:

- Lighting
- Wardrobe styling
- Composition
- Camera movement
- Set design
- Palette
- Child direction
- Editing pace

Real footage can gradually replace generated lifestyle video where commercially appropriate.

---

# 46. Future Private Label

If TinyTots launches genuine private-label clothing, video rules may expand to include:

- TinyTots garment labels
- Product design stories
- Manufacturing narratives
- Collection films
- Material storytelling
- Product-specific campaigns

Do not apply those rules before the business actually reaches that stage.

---

# 47. Performance Principle

Video should earn its cost.

Before placing a video on a page, ask:

- Does motion materially improve this section?
- Could a strong photograph communicate the same thing?
- Does video harm page speed?
- Does it distract from shopping?
- Does it improve storytelling?
- Is mobile bandwidth justified?

Do not use video merely because the asset exists.

---

# 48. Usage Restraint

A page should not automatically use HV01, CV01, EV01 and BV01 together.

Recommended principle:

Use the minimum amount of motion necessary.

Example:

Homepage:
HV01

Our Story:
EV01

Campaign landing page:
CV01

Selected transition/background:
BV01

This keeps motion purposeful.

---

# 49. Desktop/Mobile Selection

The website should serve the composition appropriate to the viewport where implementation permits.

Conceptually:

Desktop
→ HV01

Mobile
→ MV01

Do not force a poorly cropped landscape hero onto a narrow phone screen when an approved vertical composition exists.

---

# 50. Video Accessibility

Video must not create barriers.

Where appropriate:

- Provide reduced-motion behavior
- Avoid flashing content
- Maintain readable overlay contrast
- Avoid essential information existing only in video
- Ensure controls when user interaction requires them
- Provide meaningful alternatives for content-bearing video where necessary

Decorative background video may be treated differently from informational video.

---

# 51. Video QA Checklist

Before approving a new TinyTots video, confirm:

- [ ] Does the video have a defined purpose?
- [ ] Is it in the correct Video folder?
- [ ] Does it match TinyTots visual DNA?
- [ ] Does it accurately represent TinyTots as a retailer?
- [ ] Are garments free from invented TinyTots branding?
- [ ] Are children represented naturally and appropriately?
- [ ] Are faces stable?
- [ ] Are hands anatomically plausible?
- [ ] Is movement believable?
- [ ] Are garments visually stable?
- [ ] Are there random logos or text?
- [ ] Is the environment coherent?
- [ ] Is camera movement restrained?
- [ ] Is negative space preserved where required?
- [ ] Is permanent UI text absent?
- [ ] Is the aspect ratio correct?
- [ ] Is the resolution sufficient for intended use?
- [ ] Is the duration appropriate?
- [ ] Has the complete video been reviewed?
- [ ] Has looping been tested where required?
- [ ] Is a mobile version actually necessary?
- [ ] Is the production file optimized?
- [ ] Is a poster/fallback required?
- [ ] Is reduced-motion behavior considered?
- [ ] Is the master stored appropriately?
- [ ] Is the large master excluded from normal Git history?
- [ ] Does the asset create enough value to justify video?

Video supports the experience.
